KIND 1 · CHUNK
────────────────────────────────────────────────
A complete, standalone, reusable spoken expression (2–12 words).
For a chunk: text == source_text (exact contiguous transcript wording).

Hunt actively for these families:

  DISCOURSE PIVOTS
    "That said,", "Even so,", "With that in mind,",
    "Which brings me to...", "Circling back to...", "To that end,"

  STANCE MARKERS
    "To be fair,", "Frankly,", "If I'm being honest,",
    "What's striking is...", "Crucially,", "Interestingly enough,"

  HEDGES & QUALIFIERS
    "To some extent,", "Broadly speaking,", "For the most part,",
    "More often than not,", "With some caveats,", "All else being equal,"

  CONCESSION MOVES
    "I get that, but...", "Fair point, though...",
    "I don't dispute [that], but...", "You could argue..., but..."

  CLARIFICATION & REPAIR
    "What I mean by that is...", "Let me put it another way,",
    "To be more precise,", "What I'm getting at is..."

  FRAMING OPENERS
    "Here's the thing:", "The way I think about it is...",
    "Let me give you some context.", "To give you a sense of..."

  SOFTENED ASSERTIONS
    "I'd argue that...", "There's a case to be made that...",
    "My sense is that...", "It's worth noting that..."

  STRONG COLLOCATIONS (extract as one unit)
    verb+noun: "strike a balance", "draw a distinction", "bear the cost",
               "make a compelling case", "bridge the gap", "move the needle"
    adj+noun:  "a nuanced view", "a sharp contrast", "a calculated risk",
               "a common misconception", "a natural fit"

CHUNK REJECTION RULES
  ✗ Filler-only phrases with no pragmatic function
  ✗ Proper nouns, company names, domain-specific facts
  ✗ Expressions only meaningful inside this specific topic
  ✗ Near-duplicates of an already-selected candidate
  ✗ "I think", "you know", "kind of" unless pragmatic use is exceptional
  ✗ Anything below B1 unless naturalness_score = 5

KIND 3 · STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────
A productive sentence pattern capturing a PRAGMATIC MOVE. Must be
generalisable across many topics. Use 1–3 named slots in [square brackets]
with semantic names (e.g. [claim], [concession], [situation]).
Do NOT use [X] or [Y].

For a structure: text = reusable template with slots;
source_text = exact transcript wording that demonstrates the template.

Also include the pattern_family field (see schema).

Hunt actively for these families:

  CONTRAST / REFRAME
    "It's not [assumption] — it's [reality]."
    "The problem isn't [surface issue], it's [root cause]."
    "We tend to think [belief], but actually [reframing]."
    "Less about [X], more about [Y]."

  CONCESSION + PIVOT
    "While [concession], [main claim]."
    "I understand [concern], and at the same time [counter]."
    "[Concession] — that said, [pivot]."
    "Granted, [concession], but [counter-point]."

  JUSTIFIED CLAIM
    "The reason [we did X] is [reason]."
    "We chose [option] precisely because [justification]."
    "[Claim], and here's why: [reason]."
    "What drove [decision] was [factor]."

  FRAMED EXPLANATION
    "Think of [concept] as [analogy]."
    "The way [X] works is: [explanation]."
    "To give you a sense of [scale/dimension], [concrete example]."
    "The best way to describe [X] is [description]."

  HEDGED POSITION
    "[Claim], though I'd caveat that [caveat]."
    "My sense is [position], with the caveat that [uncertainty]."
    "[Observation], for the most part — with some exceptions."

  CLEFT & PSEUDO-CLEFT  ← grammar patterns worth internalising
    "What [subject] [verb] is [noun phrase]."
      e.g. "What makes this hard is the uncertainty."
    "It's [focus element] that [rest of clause]."
      e.g. "It's the execution, not the idea, that matters."
    "[Noun] is [one / the thing] that [relative clause]."
      e.g. "Trust is the one thing that you cannot fake."
    "The [noun] that [relative clause] is [result]."

  NARRATIVE ARC
    "We started with [situation], realised [insight], and ended up [outcome]."
    "What began as [initial state] turned into [result]."
    "It took [trigger] for us to realise [insight]."

  EMPHASIS STRUCTURE
    "What makes [X] [quality] is [factor]."
    "The thing that changed everything was [turning point]."
    "Of all the [things], [specific thing] stands out."

  COMPARATIVE DIMINISHMENT
    "Less [overrated quality], more [underrated quality]."
    "Not so much [rejected frame] as [accepted frame]."

STRUCTURE REJECTION RULES
  ✗ Long quotations with no abstraction (no slots = not a structure)
  ✗ Patterns tied to one topic that won't transfer
  ✗ Patterns that duplicate a chunk already captured
